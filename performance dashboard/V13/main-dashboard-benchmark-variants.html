Main Dashboard, S&P 500 Card Options

Two tiers. The strip at the top holds all three candidate cards at once, cropped to the card and scaled down, so a review call can point at one by number; clicking a card selects it. The tier below shows that card in full dashboard context: alerts, Total Value, Core/Managed, the Active Self-Directed hero, Cash, the card under review, Calibration, Active Positions and The record. The shell is written once and only the card swaps, so the thing under review is the only thing that moves. All three cards come from the benchmark entry-point study and run the performance dashboard's own seeded chart engine, so the lines are generated rather than drawn: drag across a plot to read any point along it, release to return to rest. Tapping a card anywhere outside its plot opens the performance dashboard, scoped to the selected account, which is what this card exists to do. The strip thumbnails are static.

Three independent controls. The strip picks the card design, the account chip inside the frame picks the account and rewrites every figure on the Self-Directed side, and Deposit state below picks between a funded account, an account with no deposits yet, and no self-directed account at all. The two empty states outrank the account selection: with nothing measured there are no account figures to print.

1 · Alpha Line

One line, one question: above the gold S&P axis or below it. The benchmark is the axis itself, so portfolio and S&P value appear only in the rows beneath the chart.

2 · Instrument

The performance dashboard’s own three-line chart adopted almost as-is, legend chip and left ticks included, so the card is continuous with the page it opens. It differentiates below the chart instead, in the readout strip’s larger figures.

3 · Headline

Puts the numbers first: portfolio and S&P balance sit above the chart as a headline, and the period row re-runs the same chart for YTD, 1Y or 3Y before anyone taps through. Tallest of the three.
