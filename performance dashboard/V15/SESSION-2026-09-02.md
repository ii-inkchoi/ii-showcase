## Line-box measurement correction (2026-09-02)

`main dashboard.html`'s own spacing comment previously asserted "13px paints 15, 15px paints 17" and told readers not to reintroduce 16/19. That assertion was measured against the fallback font, not Inter.

With Inter actually loaded, Chrome paints 16 at 13px, 19 at 15px, and 14 at 11px. 15/17/12 is exactly Arial's triple, the metric of this stack's fallback face. Any future line-box measurement must `await document.fonts.ready` and let the face settle before reading a rect, or it will read Arial's numbers and come back 1 to 2px short at every size.

## Closed positions get a hollow endpoint dot, open stay filled (2026-09-03)

Applied to all three `Positions vs S&P 500` charts.

**Why.** The Decisions chart plots open and closed positions together on the same axes, and `V` and `BA` each hold both an open position and a closed one. Without a distinguishing mark, that chart was drawing two lines per ticker with nothing telling them apart.

**What changed.** An open position's line still ends in a filled, glowing dot. A closed position's line now ends in a hollow ring instead.

**Measured, not assumed, before shipping it:**

- An all-hollow rendering (as the Closed screen's chart now is, since everything on it is closed) does **not** read weakly. Peak contrast of the ring came within 3% of a filled disc's, and the hole in the middle matches the ground colour exactly, so a line crossing behind a hollow endpoint is still fully occluded, same as it would be behind a filled one.
- The closed ring is **ground-filled**, not `fill:none`. Four of the nine closed endpoints have another line running through them, and a bare, transparent ring at that spot reads as a filled dot again once a second line passes under it. Filling the hole with the background colour keeps it reading as hollow regardless of what passes behind it.

**Legend sentence added to the Decisions chart's note only:** "A filled dot is still held, a hollow one is closed." Not added to the Open or Closed screens' own charts, because each of those shows only one kind of mark (all filled, all hollow), so a legend there would be explaining a distinction that is not actually on screen.
