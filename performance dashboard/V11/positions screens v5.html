Sharpe Ratio 1.05

Return earned per unit of total volatility taken. Higher means the same return was reached with a smoother ride.

Sortino Ratio 1.07

Sharpe counting only downside volatility as risk. Upside swings are not penalised, so it reads higher than Sharpe when losses are shallow.

Excess return over the benchmark per unit of deviation from it. Answers whether the active bets paid for the risk they added. Negative means they did not.

Understanding This Page ×
What This Page Shows

Your full account performance for the "All" period since inception: return vs. S&P, timing skill (TWR vs MWR), risk (drawdown, volatility), every open and closed decision, and which positions drove the result.

Why 5 Years

A short track record can't separate skill from luck. A good year or two can easily be variance. Five years is roughly the point where a return series starts to say something statistically meaningful about an active manager versus an index. The progress bar at the top tracks how close this account is to that mark, not to any performance goal.

TWR vs. MWR

Time-weighted return measures stock-picking skill. Money-weighted return reflects the timing of your deposits and withdrawals. The gap between them is timing skill, separate from picking skill.
