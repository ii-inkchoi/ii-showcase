Why the S&P 500

Most investors underperform

Over long periods, the majority of investors—professional and retail—fail to outperform the S&P 500.

~90% Underperform over 10 years Source: S&P Global
~98% Underperform over 20 years Source: S&P Global

The default assumption should be that you will not beat the market.

Why this system works

It captures winners automatically

Capital flows to the best-performing companies over time. The index is self-cleansing.

It removes most decision errors

Fewer decisions result in fewer catastrophic mistakes. Systematic discipline beats emotional agility.

It aligns with long-term compounding

Low friction, high consistency. The cost of being wrong is minimized through broad participation.

It adapts to the future

As new winners emerge, they become the index. You own tomorrow's giants by holding today's system.

You do not need to predict the future to benefit from it.

Why not other approaches

Global indexes

Broader exposure includes weaker systems, lower productivity, and structural inefficiencies. Diversification often introduces drag.

NASDAQ-100

Higher concentration in one sector. More dependent on a single cycle. Growth is non-linear and volatile.

Total market

More exposure does not equal better exposure. Smaller companies are often less durable and less capital efficient.

Thematic strategies (AI, etc.)

Requires precise prediction. Most investors get the timing or the vehicle wrong.

Most alternatives introduce either lower quality, higher risk, or reliance on being right.

The serious argument against this

AI and technological change may concentrate value into a small number of companies. In that world, diversification could dilute returns.

This is a valid concern.

The question is whether you can consistently identify and hold those companies better than a system that adapts automatically.

The position

The S&P 500 is not the goal. It is the baseline.

You deviate only if you have a system—and results—that justify it. Without that, you are making an unproven bet against the most successful economic engine in history.
