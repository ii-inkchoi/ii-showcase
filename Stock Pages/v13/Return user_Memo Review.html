Why ATZ is classified as Active

ATZ is an individual stock — owning it implies a claim that this single security can outperform the broad market through your security selection. That makes it an Active allocation by definition: you are betting on this company, not on market exposure.

The system pre-classifies all individual securities (single stocks) as Active, and all broad-market ETFs / index funds as Core / Treasury. The label follows from what you own, not from what you intend, because the asset type is what carries the edge claim.

You can't change this classification — it's structural to the asset, not a preference. If you want a different stance for this capital, the answer is to use a different instrument (e.g., a stock for Active, an ETF for Core).

Every position the system records falls into one of two categories. The classification is not a user choice — it follows from the instrument you hold, because the asset type itself determines whether an edge claim is being made.

Active allocation — assigned to individual securities (single stocks). Owning a single company implies a claim of edge: that this position can outperform the broad market through security selection. The memo records why, and the record is later measured against the S&P 500 over your stated horizon.

Core / Treasury — assigned to broadly diversified funds (ETFs, index funds). Owning these is market exposure, not a bet on any specific company — no edge claim is being made. The memo records why this capital belongs here, in this form, at this scale — for liquidity, long-term compounding, or stability.

The labels exist so positions are measured against the right standard: Active positions are assessed on outperformance, Core / Treasury positions on the role they were meant to play. If you want a different stance for this capital, the answer is to use a different instrument — not to relabel the position.

Neither label is better than the other. They describe different kinds of bets, measured by different standards.

This is a journaling and self-calibration framework, not investment advice or a recommendation. The system assigns the classification based on the instrument; it records and measures your own judgment over time.
