Aritzia Inc. (TSX: ATZ) — Investment Memo

01 — Business Overview

Aritzia is a Vancouver-founded (1984) design house and vertically integrated retailer of women's premium apparel. It designs, merchandises, and sells an in-house portfolio of exclusive brands (Wilfred, TNA, Babaton, Sunday Best, the Super Puff outerwear franchise) through its own boutiques and a single direct e-commerce channel. There is no wholesale and there are no third-party brands. Aritzia controls design, pricing, allocation, and the customer relationship end to end, positioning itself as "Everyday Luxury": a tier above mall fast-fashion (H&M, Zara) and below true luxury.

FY2026 was a record year. Net revenue reached C$3,702M, up 35%, capping a four-year revenue CAGR of ~25% (from C$1,495M in FY2022). Gross profit was C$1,661M (44.9% margin), operating profit C$524M (14.2% margin), net income C$382M (10.3% margin), and diluted EPS C$3.20 versus C$1.78 the prior year. Comparable sales grew ~27%, positive double-digit across every channel and geography.

The structural story is geographic. The US is now the larger and faster market: US revenue C$2,275M (61% of total, +38%) versus Canada C$1,427M (39%, +24%). The fleet ended FY2026 at 144 boutiques (76 US, 68 Canada) after opening 14 and repositioning four, including a Manhattan Flatiron flagship. Canada is effectively built out (count flat for five years); the entire unit-growth engine is the US, where new boutiques recoup build-out capital in under a year and run ahead of plan. E-commerce contributed C$1,295M (35% of revenue), headed toward a ~45% target.

The balance sheet is pristine: C$592M cash, no bank debt (only IFRS-16 lease liabilities ~C$996M), free cash flow ~C$538M (14.5% FCF margin), ROIC ~20% / ROE ~28%. CEO Jennifer Wong (since 2022) runs operations; founder Brian Hill is Executive Chair. A dual-class structure concentrates voting control with the founder group.

02 — Classification

Emerging Compounder.

Aritzia has the architecture of a compounder: a differentiated in-house brand portfolio with pricing power; ~20% ROIC and ~28% ROE; net-cash funding; a repeatable unit-growth algorithm (open a US box, recoup capital in <12 months, layer comps on top); and an owner-operator at the helm. It falls short of "established compounder" on track-record consistency: the model stumbled visibly in FY2024 (comps went negative −1%, operating margin halved to 6.8%, EPS fell to C$0.69) on excess inventory and markdowns. The recovery since (FY2025 EPS C$1.78, FY2026 C$3.20) has been emphatic, but two clean years do not yet prove through-cycle durability. The US runway is large and early (76 boutiques against frequently cited white-space of 150+); that is the "emerging" part. The prize is mostly ahead, not banked.

Not a situational mispricing (nothing is broken or misunderstood). Not speculative (the economics are proven and cash-generative). A high-quality, early-innings franchise.

03 — Thesis and Structural Decomposition

Thesis. Aritzia is a US growth story in its middle innings. The US flywheel (flagship-led brand-building, app-driven engagement, and disciplined new-box openings that pay back in under a year) can roughly double the US fleet while e-commerce climbs toward 45% of revenue. Revenue compounds mid-to-high teens, margins expand on occupancy leverage and improving initial mark-up (IMU), EPS outgrows revenue, and at a sensible entry price the return clears the hurdle with room to spare.

Structural decomposition of the five-year return:

  • Revenue ≈ unit growth (~12–13 new US boutiques/year, low-teens square-footage growth) + comparable sales (decelerating from ~27% toward guided mid-teens) + e-commerce penetration. FY2027 guided to C$4.4–4.6B (+19–24%).
  • Margin ≈ gross-margin expansion (IMU + occupancy leverage, guided +150–200bps in FY2027) net of ~50bps incremental tariff/de-minimis drag, plus SG&A leverage from the "smart spending" initiative.
  • Capital returns ≈ buybacks (C$207M in FY2026; no dividend) modestly shrinking the count against stock-comp dilution.
  • Multiple ≈ the swing factor; at an C$80 entry (~25x trailing) the rating embeds far less optimism than the live ~47x.

The load-bearing assumption: that Aritzia can roughly double its US boutique base (76 → 150+) without per-store productivity or brand desirability eroding — i.e., US demand is supply-constrained, not the reverse. Every other input is downstream of this. The model works only if the marginal new US store, increasingly in smaller and less-proven markets, keeps approaching the productivity and sub-12-month payback of the existing fleet. If new-store productivity mean-reverts toward ordinary specialty-retail levels, the unit-growth algorithm degrades, comps normalize, and the multiple compresses at the same time. Buying at a conservative multiple is the defense against that convergence.

04 — Why Now

Operational. FY2026 (reported 7 May 2026) was a clean beat-and-raise. Aritzia hit its FY2027 revenue target a full year early, posted +27% comps, grew US revenue 38%, and expanded adjusted EBITDA margin ~260bps despite ~260bps of tariff and de-minimis drag. Direct evidence of pricing power and cost discipline. It launched the Aritzia app (deeper engagement and first-party data), renewed its NCIB, and guided FY2027 to another ~20%+ revenue year with gross margin up 150–200bps. Execution and the US flywheel are firing.

Valuation (the reason this memo is a buy). At the illustrative C$80 entry, the franchise trades around 25x trailing / ~18x forward earnings, roughly a market multiple for a business compounding revenue ~20% with ~20% ROIC. The "why now" is the rare alignment of a proven, accelerating operating model with an entry price that does not require the premium rating to persist. The base case clears the hurdle on earnings growth alone; multiple stability is upside, not a requirement.

06 — Intrinsic Value

Method. A multi-stage DCF on a fashion retailer with double-digit comp volatility produces false precision; terminal value would dominate and rest on the same unprovable long-run assumptions. I use an EPS-and-exit-multiple framework over five years (FY2026 → FY2031), cross-checked against free cash flow, because the equity return here is mechanically forward earnings × terminal multiple, and because it forces the multiple, the dominant swing factor, into the open.

Base inputs: FY2026 diluted EPS C$3.20; ~115M diluted shares (held roughly flat as buybacks offset stock comp).

Three scenarios (FY2031):

Scenario Rev. CAGR FY31 Revenue Net margin FY31 EPS Exit P/E FY31 price 5-yr IRR from C$80
Bull 20% ~C$9.2B 13.0% ~C$10.7 32x ~C$342 ~34%
Base 15% ~C$7.4B 11.5% ~C$7.4 26x ~C$192 ~19%
Bear 8% ~C$5.4B 9.5% ~C$4.4 16x ~C$70 ~ −3%

Conservative intrinsic value. The hurdle-clearing entry price for each scenario (FY31 price ÷ 2.49) is bull C$137, base C$77, bear C$28. Probability-weighting (30% / 45% / 25%) gives a hurdle-clearing entry of ~C$80 and a conservative IV band of C$80–100.

Margin of safety. At the C$80 entry against the C$80–100 conservative IV, the margin of safety is adequate (roughly 0–20%): the base case clears the hurdle on earnings alone, the bull case clears it comfortably, and only the bear case (which embeds both a growth stall and a de-rating to 16x) loses money, and modestly. The asymmetry is favorable: ~+34% / +19% / −3% across bull / base / bear.

07 — Probability of Clearing the Hurdle

To clear 20% annualized from C$80, the price must reach ~C$96 in 1 year, ~C$138 in 3 years, and ~C$199 in 5 years.

Horizon Target price Probability Reasoning
1-year ~C$96 ~55% A +20% move is well within range given FY2027 guided +19–24% revenue and continued beat-and-raise momentum; the conservative entry multiple removes de-rating risk as the main obstacle.
3-year (binding) ~C$138 ~55% Bull and base scenarios both clear (base reaches ~C$135 on +19% IRR; bull ~C$209). Only the bear case fails. Weighted central tendency sits just above the hurdle.
5-year ~C$199 ~58% Longer compounding lets earnings growth carry the return even with moderate multiple compression; base case lands ~C$192, essentially at target, with the bull tail providing cushion.

Conclusion — binding rule applied: 3-year probability ~55%, which is ≥ 50%.

TRIAGE: PROMOTE

At a conservative entry, Aritzia offers a proven, accelerating US growth model with ~20% ROIC at a multiple that does not require optimism to persist. The base case clears the 20% hurdle on earnings growth alone; multiple stability and the bull tail are upside. Promote.

08 — Where Deeper Work Would Move the Probability

Because the verdict is Promote, the following work would sharpen conviction and position sizing before committing capital. Each item targets the load-bearing assumption or a kill criterion directly.

  1. US store-cohort economics (highest priority). Obtain or triangulate sales-per-square-foot and payback by boutique vintage and market tier. Confirming that newer, smaller-market US stores hold fleet-level productivity would move the bull/base weighting up and raise the 3-year probability above 60%. This is the single most thesis-relevant diligence item (kill #2).
  2. Comp decomposition. Split comps into traffic vs. ticket vs. new-client acquisition. Comps driven by durable, app-led new-client growth justify a higher sustainable multiple and de-risk kill #1.
  3. Tariff pass-through elasticity. Model gross-margin sensitivity to the 10% US tariff regime and de-minimis suspension at varying pass-through rates. FY2026 absorbed ~260bps of EBITDA drag without margin loss; quantifying remaining pricing headroom bounds the bear case.
  4. E-commerce contribution margin as the mix moves toward 45%; confirm it is accretive (not dilutive) to consolidated margin at scale, which underpins the margin-expansion leg of the thesis.
  5. Capital-allocation cadence. Map the buyback pace against stock-comp dilution and the >C$1B projected cash build; confirm management converts the cash pile into per-share value rather than letting it idle.

Position-sizing implication: start at a half-weight on the Promote, with the cohort-economics work (item 1) as the trigger to scale to full weight.
