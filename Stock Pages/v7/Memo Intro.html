Memo · Intro

Conviction, written down.

Most apps are built for the trade. A price, a chart, a green button. II is built for the decision.

Before you ever act, you write or read the memo. Nine sections that force the questions that matter: what you're betting on, what would prove you wrong, what it's worth, and the odds. When the trade comes, it's the conclusion of an argument, not an impulse.

The memo is a structured investment case for a single company. What the business is, why it might be worth owning, what would prove you wrong, what it's worth, and the odds it meets your goal. It ends in one word: Promote or Pass. It's the discipline professionals use, built for you.

Anatomy of a Memo
01
Business Overview
What this company actually does, and how it makes money.
02
Classification
What kind of investment this is. A great business and a great investment are not the same thing.
03
Thesis
The case for owning it. The single assumption everything rests on.
04
Why Now
Why this is worth your attention today, not just someday.
05
Kill Criteria
The signals that would tell you to sell. Decided in advance, while you're calm. Not in a panic.
06
Intrinsic Value
What it's actually worth, across a good, expected, and bad outcome.
07
Probability
The honest odds it meets your return goal. This is where the memo decides: Promote or Pass.
08
Where to Dig Deeper
If it's a Promote, the homework that would raise your conviction before you commit.
09
Verify
Every key number, traceable to the source. Trust, then check.
The Verdict
Promote. The case clears your goal. Worth a position.
Pass. Not at this price.
Every memo ends in one word. No maybes. The point of the work is to reach a stand you can defend, then act on it.
