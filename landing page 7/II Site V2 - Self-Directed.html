NO. 01 · THE RECORD

A thesis without a stated way to be wrong is an opinion.

Before an active order, the position is classified, its role and size are set, and you state your probability of clearing your hurdle. The investment case can be added later, and when it is, it carries the date it was written, not the date of the order. The system does not invent a history you did not create.

The case states what would prove it wrong. Once sealed, it locks. Editing creates a new version. The original is never overwritten.

You will read it later and disagree with yourself. That is the record working.

NO. 03 · THE REVIEW

A record is not written once.

Writing it is the first pass. As the thesis is tested it is revised, version by version, and every capital decision answers to the record, never the other way around.

01

Record

The first time, you fill out the record: classification, sizing, confidence, idea origin, and the investment case.

02

Review

As the thesis is tested, you revise it section by section. Each revision becomes a new version. The new version is dated. The previous version remains.

03

Capital decision

Your decision to initiate, increase, or reduce always follows the record, never the other way around.

NO. 04 · CALIBRATION

Confidence, scored against outcome.

Every allocator carries a private conviction about how often they are right. Almost none has ever measured it. The number is almost always worse than the belief.

Each decision is recorded as a probability against a horizon, then scored against what occurred. As horizons close, stated probabilities and actual outcomes become comparable. Outcome and decision quality begin to separate. The record does not care what you believed.

A sound decision can lose. A reckless one can pay off. The outcome alone never tells you which you made.

There are only two kinds of allocators: those who have been humbled, and those who are about to be. The only question is what happens next.

NO. 05 · THE DEVELOPMENT

The portfolio is an artifact. The allocator is the product.

The record, the benchmark, the review, and the score are not features. They are training. What they develop is specific: calibration, probabilistic reasoning, position sizing, updating when the evidence changes, learning from error, decision quality.

The system does not try to improve your outcomes. It tries to improve the decision-maker. Outcomes are downstream.

We promise no outcome. We hold one belief: people who consistently make higher-quality allocation decisions tend to achieve better long-term outcomes. Not always. Not immediately. But over time.

A rigorous record may teach some investors that most of their capital belongs in the default. That is not failure. That is the system working. The highest expression of allocation is knowing when you can beat the market, and when you cannot.
