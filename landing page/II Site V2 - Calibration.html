/01Premise

Confidence is a claim.
Reality is the test.

Most investors carry strong opinions and weak feedback. Convictions are stated loudly, outcomes are reviewed selectively, and the gap between stated confidence and realized outcome is never measured.

Calibration is the measurement. Every memo states a confidence (10–90%) and a time horizon. Over years, every prediction is compared to what actually happened. Your judgment becomes a number on the record.

/02Mechanism

Three measurements,
compounded over decades.

Accuracy
Across all sealed memos, how often does stated confidence match realized outcome? A well-calibrated investor stating 70% confidence should be right 70% of the time.
Predictions
Every closed position carries its original sealed forecast. No hindsight rewriting. No survivor bias. The record is what you actually committed to.
Next Judgment
What the calibration record argues you should adjust. Systematic overconfidence in compounders. Underconfidence in mispricings. The pattern, made visible.

Drill into any band to see the underlying memos. Closed memos carry their original confidence claim. The record is permanent.

You don't become a better investor by being more confident. You become a better investor by knowing when your confidence is wrong.
/04Doctrine

Intelligence as status.
Measured, not claimed.

In most platforms, confidence is performance. The loudest opinion is the most valued. The track record, if kept at all, is selective.

Here, calibration is the metric. Over a decade, every sealed memo becomes data. Patterns emerge: where you reliably see what others don't, and where you systematically don't see what's there.

This is the long arc. The portfolio compounds capital. The calibration record compounds judgment. The investor who keeps both improves at both.
