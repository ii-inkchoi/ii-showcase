## band-1.jpg / band-2.jpg / band-3.jpg (archived 2026-09-08)

The first three 08 bands. Archived rather than deleted, because the reason they failed is
worth keeping: they were picked as grey architectural TEXTURE, on the same rule as the page's
grounds, and 08's bands are pictures rather than grounds. A picture has nothing standing on
it, so it has to have a subject, and these had none. band-3 was a street with tram overhead
lines that reads as Eastern European, attached to a section about Canadian consumer lending.

Replaced with real photography from the Intelligent Investing library, one subject per
operating business:

| band | business | shows |
|------|----------|-------|
| band-1 | Wealth | a grid of filing drawers: records, and keeping account of them |
| band-2 | Payments | ordered rows of line cards: plant that carries other people's volume |
| band-3 | Consumer lending | a shop aisle and a shopper: ordinary spending |

Two rejected on the way, after being picked from thumbnails and then checked at full size,
which is the only reason they were caught:

- a mid-century cartographer at a drafting table. Real, and genuinely absorbed in difficult
  work, but plainly a 1950s archive frame, so it reads as heritage rather than as a business
  operating now.
- a night apartment block with air-conditioners on every balcony. Real, but not Canadian by
  any reading, and it came from a marketing reel with guide lines already burnt into it.

None of these depict Orion's own premises. They are context, not depiction, and nothing on
the page claims otherwise. Real photography of Intelligent Investing, Carta Worldwide and
Mogo replaces them when it exists.

## ground-veil-bw.jpg (archived 2026-09-08)

02's ground in black and white, derived from the concrete stand-in. Archived when the page
went to muted colour: the file measured saturation 0.0, so no colour was recoverable from it,
and as the page's largest dark surface it was what made the page look half in colour and half
not.

Replaced by a ground baked from a warm source, ernest-mombrun, chosen for a reason that only
applies to this surface: 02's ground is an inverted negative, and inverting flips temperature.
A cool source returns warm, which is the banned golden tint. This one measures +23.9 warm at
the sensor and inverts to cool.

## hero-vessel.jpg and hero-toronto.jpg (archived 2026-09-08)

Two hero frames replaced by `hero-district.jpg`, a street-level financial-district canyon that
is the first candidate to pass every measurable criterion in `_build/check-hero.py` and the eye
check with it.

**hero-vessel.jpg**, a container vessel at sea, stood in for the load-bearing direction and was
technically strong: luminance mean 38.7 and type contrasts of 8.72 / 6.33 / 12.26, roughly
double every floor. It was never Canadian, which was the point of the brief.

**hero-toronto.jpg**, a Toronto dusk street, was the frame that proved the direction exists and
could not ship as one. Two reasons:

- Sun Life's logo is legible on a tower in the middle of the frame. A Canadian financial
  institution's mark in an issuer's hero lets a reader infer a relationship. Cropping it out
  needed a 6x upscale from an 800px source, so there was no way round it.
- 800px wide, a 3.2x upscale. It survived only because the hero bakes in blur and grain, and
  that is not a thing to rely on twice.

It did settle two questions worth keeping. The frame's dusk read **R-B +30.1** raw before the
window pulled it to -0.2, which is why temperature is checked after the window and not before.
And it is why the sourcing brief now prefers `overcast` over `blue hour`: dusk goes golden in a
Canadian downtown too, from the tungsten street lighting.

**ground-district.jpg** was 02's ground for one pass. It looked up at a glass facade, and the

with the facade's diagonals cutting across the sheet's horizontals. Replaced by

**pic-files.jpg** was 03's picture, bundles of filed paper. Replaced by `pic-ledgers.jpg`,

- ring binders at 2225px, the best resolution and temperature of the set, but the handwritten

  stopped being background.
- the National Library of Norway's reading hall. Beautiful and institutional, and three things
  against it: the file is already saturation 0.0 so it cannot join the colour policy, it
  measures luminance 157 which is far too bright for this slot, and it reads as a public

- stacks of loose paper. The earlier verdict held up in the slot: the sheets lean and spill, so
  it reads as an unprocessed backlog rather than as records kept, which is the wrong implication
  on an investor page.

## pic-ledgers.jpg (archived 2026-09-08, same day)

03's picture for one pass, shelved bound ledgers. It failed for the reason 08's bands had
already taught and I did not apply here: **a picture has to keep its subject at the size the
layout renders it.** The spines are so uniform and tightly packed that at 386px they stop
reading as records and become a corrugated stripe pattern. Judged as a full frame it looked
right, which is exactly the trap RULES.md 4b was written about.

Replaced by `pic-stacks.jpg`, stacked folders, where individual files, clips and edges stay
separable at that size. The reservation on the record: leaning sheets can read as an
unprocessed backlog rather than as records kept, which is why the crop is biased to the
squarest part of the frame. It is also why this frame was refused twice before being chosen.

Its window is lower than the ledgers used, 34 to 214 at gamma 0.80 rather than 40 to 240 at
0.65, because this frame arrives far brighter and 03 sits on cream, where a near-white picture
stops reading as an object at all.

## hero-district.jpg (archived 2026-09-08) and hero-vessel.jpg (restored the same day)

The hero went to a street-level financial-district canyon and came back to the vessel.

The vessel wins on measurement by a distance. Contrasts of **8.72 / 6.33 / 12.26** against
floors of 4.5 / 3.0 / 4.5, on a ground at luminance 38.7 and R-B -17.9. The district frame
passed too, but at **4.94 / 4.32 / 5.42** it lived close to its floors, because an overcast sky
is a bright flat field and the type has to sit on it.

Everything else sourced fails outright, and the checker says why: golden casts of +5.1, +9.8 and
+5.2 after the window, or luminance between 55 and 91. Only one other frame passed clean, a
facade close-up, and it is out of focus.

**What the vessel costs is the Canadian claim.** It does not make one. That is the accepted
trade, not an oversight: the brief wants a Canadian frame and no frame sourced so far both
carries one and holds the type. The one that proves the city photographically, a canyon with
the CN Tower in the middle of it, is archived here as `ground-canyon.jpg` and is worth
revisiting whenever a hero with better headroom turns up.

## pic-stacks.jpg (archived 2026-09-08, same day it was installed)

03's picture for one pass, stacked folders, refused on sight for exactly the reservation that
had been written down twice before it was installed: the sheets lean and spill, a blue loose
page and cardboard boxes are in frame, and it reads as an unprocessed backlog rather than as
records kept. Installing it anyway, with the objection already on the record, was the mistake.

That closes the records subject family for this slot. Six frames, six different failures:
concrete (no subject), a hand writing in a notebook (too literal), ring binders (non-Latin
labels legible), a library reading hall (reads public, luminance 157), bound ledgers (the
subject dissolves into a stripe pattern at 386px), and these folders (backlog). The pattern
is not bad luck. A photograph of records is necessarily a photograph of stacked paper, and
stacked paper reads as a texture when it is tidy and as a backlog when it is not. At 386px
there is almost no gap between those two.

Replaced by `pic-night.jpg`, a night financial district frame, and in colour by instruction.
