/01 Premise

Most investors lose to the market they could have just bought.

The S&P 500 is the floor. Buying it requires no skill, no research, no system. It is the most boring outcome available to a long-term investor.

The data on professionals attempting to beat it is consistent across decades.

/02 Mechanism

Every dollar carries its shadow.

For every deposit you make — every dollar that enters Self-Directed positions — the system holds a parallel ledger.

Same dollars. Same timestamps. Held in SPY.

No selection bias. No survivorship. No hindsight. Just what would have happened if you had bought the index instead.

Per-tranche shadow Each deposit and trade is mirrored in SPY at the same time, with the same dollars. Continuous
Selection bias No cherry-picking of start dates or comparison periods. The shadow exists for the full life of every dollar. None
Survivorship Closed positions remain in the calibration record. Past losses are not hidden by current winners. None
Fees & FX Net of all commissions (zero), FX markups (zero), and management fees (zero for Self-Directed). Net
Reporting Three numbers visible at all times: Portfolio · S&P 500 · Difference. Live

/04 Doctrine

The benchmark is not a competitor.
It is the test.

In most platforms, performance is celebrated when up and hidden when down. The number you see is the friendlier one.

Here, the comparison is constant. The honest gap to passive is visible at every moment.

Most users will not beat the index over a decade. That is not a failure of the system. It is the system working as designed — surfacing reality rather than narrative.

The benchmark turns activity into evidence. Whether the evidence supports continuing or reverting to a disciplined core is up to the operator.
