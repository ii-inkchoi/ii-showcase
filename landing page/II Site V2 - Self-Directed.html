No. 01The Memo

Every position begins with a memo.

A sealed record of the reasoning behind a position: classification, sizing, confidence, where the idea came from, and the case for it. Once sealed, it locks. Editing it creates a new version. The original is never overwritten.

You will read it later and disagree with yourself. That is the record working.

No. 02The Process

A memo is not written once.

Writing it is the first pass. As the thesis is tested it is re-underwritten, version by version, and every capital decision answers to the record, not the other way around.

Write

The first time, you fill out the memo: classification, sizing, confidence, idea origin, and the investment case.

Re-underwrite

As the thesis is tested, you revise it section by section. Each revision becomes a new version.

Capital Decision

The capital decision to initiate, increase, or reduce always follows the memo, never the other way around.

No. 03Accountability

Every dollar is benchmarked. Every forecast is scored.

Benchmark

Every position, measured against the S&P 500, dollar for dollar. The gap is shown plainly, including when negative.

Calibration

Stated confidence, scored against what actually happened. Most active investors are overconfident, and the record proves it.

Active Positions

What you hold, ordered by weight. Each position is open, measured, and accountable to the memo that opened it.

Every holding has a Position Page with alerts, notes, performance, research, news, market data, and full history. Names under review are staged before capital is committed, and Fiscal.ai research is included with membership.

No. 04Account Types
TFSATax-free
Pros

Growth and withdrawals are completely tax-free. The annual contribution limit is $7,000 (2025), plus any unused room from prior years, which carries forward indefinitely with no expiry. Funds stay accessible without penalties or age limits.

Cons

Annual contribution caps limit how much can be invested each year, and withdrawals reduce available room until the next calendar year.

RRSPTax-deferred
Pros

Contributions reduce taxable income in the current year, or can be carried to a future year, and growth is tax-deferred until withdrawal. The annual limit is 18% of earned income, up to $32,490 (2025), plus any unused room, which carries forward automatically.

Cons

Withdrawals are fully taxed as income. Early withdrawals permanently reduce contribution room, except under the Home Buyers' Plan or Lifelong Learning Plan. The account must convert to a RRIF by age 71, ending tax-deferred compounding.

Use case

Commonly used by higher-income investors reducing taxable income during peak earning years while building long-term wealth.

Non-RegisteredTaxable
Pros

Unlimited contribution capacity, with no withdrawal limits or age restrictions. Realized capital losses can offset taxable capital gains.

Cons

Investment income and capital gains are taxed each year, reducing long-term compounding. There is no tax-sheltering or contribution carry-forward, and no structural advantage over registered accounts.

Use case

Typically used only after maximizing TFSA and RRSP contributions, or for short-term investing where preserving registered room is a priority.
