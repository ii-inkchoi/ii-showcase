The shadow of the S&P 500

Why every active position deserves to be measured against the index it could have replaced — and why most platforms hide the comparison.
PublishedJune 8, 2026
Reading time5 min
SectionDoctrine

Every dollar you invest competes against an alternative version of itself. The version that bought SPY on the same day and held.

That alternative is the floor. It requires no skill, no research, no system. It is the most boring outcome available to a long-term investor with disposable capital. And for the overwhelming majority of professionals, it is also the outcome they fail to beat.

The data on this is consistent across decades and methodologies. Over 10 years, roughly 85% of active managers underperform the S&P 500. Over 20 years, that figure climbs to around 95%. These are highly trained professionals with research teams, dedicated infrastructure, and information access that retail investors cannot replicate.

The benchmark is not a competitor. It is the test.

Why most platforms hide the comparison

If the benchmark is the test, you would expect every investing platform to display it prominently. Most do not. Some display it on dedicated screens you have to find. Many display it only on aggregate, not per-position. A few show it but only when convenient — visible during gains, dim during losses.

The reason is structural. Platforms make money when you trade. The benchmark, if shown honestly, would tell most users that their trading is destroying wealth. The comparison the customer needs is the comparison the platform must hide.

Per-tranche shadow

Intelligent Investing inverts this. Every dollar that enters a Self-Directed position is mirrored, at the same moment, by a parallel ledger holding SPY. Same dollars. Same timestamps. Same fees.

The result is not a hypothetical comparison. It is the actual gap between the choice you made and the boring alternative. Available continuously. Visible at every level — portfolio, position, tranche, decision.

What this looks like in practice

Open the Performance Dashboard. Three numbers:

  • Portfolio return — what you actually earned
  • S&P 500 return — what holding the index would have earned
  • Difference — alpha or shortfall, not hidden

Drill into any position to see the same three numbers at the position level. Drill into any tranche to see them at the tranche level. The comparison is recursive and complete.

The honest gap

For most users, the honest gap will not be positive. That is not a failure of the system. It is the system working as designed: surfacing reality rather than narrative.

What you do with that gap is up to you. Many investors, once they see it consistently, choose to allocate more to the managed core and less to active positions. Others double down, treating each shortfall as data to improve. Both responses are valid. What is not valid is operating without knowing.
